This [poster](https://danielrshabib.github.io/files/naranjo2026why-poster.pdf) explored why people in loud live music settings often do not use hearing protection despite risks of noise-induced hearing loss. We interviewed 72 adult patrons (median age 28 years) in a high-noise entertainment district and identified common barriers and motivators influencing behavior. The most frequent themes for personal non-use were apathy or dismissiveness (27.8%) and gaps in perceived risk and knowledge (23.6%), while perceptions of others’ behavior were driven by similar knowledge gaps (43.1%) and comfort or design barriers (33.3%). Key motivators for using hearing protection included education (27.8%), prior hearing loss (12.5%), and free distribution of earplugs (11.1%). These findings highlight actionable opportunities for education, improved device design, and venue-based interventions to promote hearing protection and prevent long-term hearing damage.
